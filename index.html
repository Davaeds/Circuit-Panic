How to Wire

  • Kill the power first. In this game, wires can't be touched while the breaker is on.
  • Black is hot, white is neutral, green (or bare) is ground. Red usually carries a switched hot or the travelers between 3-way switches.
  • Hot goes to brass, neutral goes to silver. The silver screw feeds the socket shell, the part your fingers touch.
  • A switch breaks the hot, never the neutral. Otherwise the socket stays live even when the light is off.
  • Ground the metal. The green wire runs from the panel's ground bar to the green screw on the box. It carries no current unless something goes wrong, and then it trips the breaker.
  • Neutral and ground meet only in the panel. Joining them anywhere else puts normal current on the ground.
  • On a 3-way, the dark screw is COMMON. The two brass screws are TRAVELERS.
  • Never let hot touch neutral or ground directly. With nothing in between, that's a short: fault current, an arc, and a tripped breaker.

In a puzzle: drag from one screw to another to run a wire (or tap one screw, then another), then wrap it round the screw clockwise with the brass grip. Two wires on one screw get pigtailed with a wire nut. Tap a wire to cut it. B flips the breaker, S flips the switch, T picks up the neon tester.

This game simplifies things for learning. Your instructor and the NEC have the final word.
